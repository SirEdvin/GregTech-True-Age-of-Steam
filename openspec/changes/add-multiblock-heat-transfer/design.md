## Context

See `proposal.md` for motivation and the three capability specs for behavior. The user's clarification defines **capacity as the safe energy threshold in J**, not physical heat capacity in J/K or a hard storage cap. Zero **stored heat** means 300 K; it does not mean zero capacity. Capacity and maximum safe temperature are abstract subclass getters derived from the current formed structure, without public setters. Stored heat can exceed capacity: this starts reversible melting and a 40-game-tick countdown. Cooling back within the safe limit stops melting and resets the countdown; sustained overheating explodes the controller and its own heat hatches.

The controller and failure responsibility boundaries are settled. The controller owns its countdown; other unloaded structure parts or hatches do not pause it. Destruction is limited to the controller and its own heat hatches with explosion sounds and no collateral blast effects. The HV–LuV hatch tiers replace the flat conductance with 2/8/32/128 J/(K * update), selected solely by the currently sending hatch; the receiver's tier does not throttle transfer. Remaining lifecycle bookkeeping is an implementation and verification concern, not another product-design blocker.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- A small reusable API, one authoritative controller energy field, one shared hatch implementation with HV–LuV variants, and a bounded network algorithm with independently testable math/path rules.
- No energy duplication from shared endpoints, cyclic paths, ticking order, stale topology, or load/unload transitions.
- A live hatch menu that works even when the controller is not instantiated on the client.
- A controller-owned melting state that permits cooling rescue and cannot be postponed by repeatedly adding more heat.
- An end-to-end test fixture using opt-in development controllers without changing shipped production multiblocks.

**Non-Goals:**
- Physical material thermodynamics, heat loss to vents/ambient, below-ambient cooling, boiling mechanics, or conversion of existing boiler heat levels. The requested melting/explosion behavior is in scope.
- Recipe capabilities, recipe modifiers, a new heat source/sink multiblock, custom pipe blocks, wireless links, cover-aware insulation, or survival crafting progression.
- A general transaction framework, universal Forge heat capability, GT pipe-network integration, or dependency/build-plugin upgrades.
- Extra heat-preserving item data for broken and replaced controllers.

## Decisions

### 1. Controller-owned store and explicit abstract characteristics

Proposed new types (not existing symbols):
- `api/IHeatMachine.java`: `getStoredHeat()`, `getHeatCapacity()`, `getTemperature()`, `getMaxTemperature()`, and `changeHeat(double deltaJoules, boolean simulate)` returning the accepted signed delta; expose read-only melting state/countdown for hatch display, without setters for that state.
- `machines/shared/heat/HeatMultiblockMachine.java`: abstract subclass of `WorkableMultiblockMachine` implementing the interface. `getHeatCapacity()` and `getMaxTemperature()` remain abstract and structure-derived; provide final standard temperature derivation and authoritative heat mutation. Preserve the existing holder/varargs constructor pattern so future recipe-controller subclasses can reuse it.
- `machines/shared/heat/HeatTransfer.java`: a small pure numerical helper shared by exchange and tests, not a separate service hierarchy.

Use a private `double storedHeat` in a correctly inherited managed field holder, persisted on the controller. Expose no generated setter. Double precision permits small equilibrium transfers without an integer-Joule stall. Validate finite positive capacity, finite maximum safe temperature above 300 K, and finite positive conversion slope for formed structures. Do not call abstract subclass getters from the superclass constructor: initialization of subclass state is not complete there. Validate when a formed structure's values are available and before mutation or exchange; invalid definitions fail closed and produce an unavailable status. Being overcapacity is a valid, dangerous thermal state, not a malformed definition: do not exclude melting controllers from cooling exchange.

Subclasses derive both characteristics from the formed structure; changes in size/components can change them. Reevaluate characteristics after formation/reformation of surviving controllers and preserve the same stored joules. Never preserve temperature by adding/removing heat or discard heat above a reduced capacity. An overcapacity result enters melting; returning within valid safe limits cancels it. A casing break while already melting immediately explodes the controller and its own heat hatches, so subsequent reformation cannot rescue it. Treat an unavailable structure during loading separately from an actually broken casing or a zero-capacity valid machine.

Normalize persisted heat independently of safe capacity: absent/non-finite/negative values become zero; every finite nonnegative value is retained, including above-capacity heat. Do not sanitize away overheating during load. Nondestructive formation loss does not reset stored heat. Persist active melting state and the remaining countdown alongside energy; record authoritative countdown changes as dirty state. Positive mutation is not capacity-limited; extraction cannot exceed stored joules, and requests producing non-finite state are rejected. Mark accepted changes dirty and reevaluate melting after real mutations. Simulation reports the feasible delta without changing heat or the countdown. Client mutation attempts and non-finite requests return zero. No setter for temperature, capacity, or maximum temperature is added.

Use `T = 300 + (Q / C) * (M - 300)`, where Q is stored J, C is safe capacity J, and M is maximum safe K. The formula also applies when `Q > C`, producing `T > M`. Compare stored heat with capacity for the melting boundary rather than rounded UI temperatures. The derived slope `s = (M - 300) / C` has units K/J; its reciprocal is an effective J/K used only for reasoning, not the public meaning of capacity.

**Alternative rejected:** inherit `PassiveCoolingMachine` or put energy into its recipe logic. That would couple heat to cooling coils, idle regeneration, and recipe consumption that the user did not request. Direct temperature mutation and a real-world J/K public capacity model contradict the clarified example.

### 2. Non-shared hatch with no independent energy storage

Propose `machines/parts/HeatHatchMachine.java`, extending the inspected `TieredPartMachine` and implementing `IFancyUIMachine`, with the holder/tier constructor and no tier setter. It returns false from `canShared()`. A single owner resolver is reused by the network and display code and validates exactly one owning controller, current loaded block-entity identity, a formed multiblock, and a valid heat implementation. Follow upstream lifecycle hooks and invoke their superclass implementations.

Register HV, EV, IV, and LuV variants in `TrueSteamMachines`, with proposed IDs `gttruesteam:hv_heat_hatch`, `gttruesteam:ev_heat_hatch`, `gttruesteam:iv_heat_hatch`, and `gttruesteam:luv_heat_hatch`. Replace the earlier unimplemented bare `heat_hatch` registration; no save migration is needed for an unshipped proposal. Reuse the inspected addon-owned `GTTrueSteam.REGISTRATE.machine(...)` pattern with a tier-capturing factory rather than assuming an upstream helper binds the addon namespace. All variants share a single `HEAT` ability in proposed `TrueSteamPartAbilities.java`. Future patterns opt in through that ability; do not modify generic GTCEu auto-abilities or current addon multiblock patterns. Supply tier-identifiable models/items, display names, coefficient tooltips, and creative availability through the existing registration/data pipeline. No separate input/output variants, electrical power draw, voltage-overload rules, or extra survival recipes are introduced.

Only each hatch's designated front face conducts, using the normal hatch-facing convention. Both source and destination faces must meet the path. Rotation changes connectivity on the next discovery update. Covers do not add a new thermal blocking mechanic in this scope. Retained proposal default: adjacent hatches connect directly only front-to-front; otherwise every intermediate position must be a computer heat vent. Hatches terminate paths and are never intermediate conducting blocks.

**Alternative rejected:** a hatch-local buffer or shared hatch permits ownership ambiguity and copied energy. Borrowing fluid/item abilities risks making unrelated existing patterns accept the hatch.

### 3. Fresh bounded vent discovery at each transfer update

Propose `machines/shared/heat/HeatNetwork.java` for a bounded breadth-first search with a narrow block/endpoint lookup boundary for tests. Start at the source hatch at distance zero and seed traversal only through its front-adjacent position. Face-adjacent destinations increase distance by one; accept a destination hatch only when approached through its conducting front face. A destination is reachable at distance 1 through 32 inclusive. A distance-32 hatch can be accepted, but traversal does not expand beyond the depth limit. Traverse only the exact `GTBlocks.COMPUTER_HEAT_VENT` block, with visited-position tracking and shortest-path discovery. This definition includes both endpoint boundary steps; there can be at most 31 intermediate vents on a shortest path of 32 steps. The user confirmed shortest connected path, not a spatial radius.

Check loaded chunks before any block-state, block-entity, or owner lookup. Do not request chunk tickets or allow lookup helpers to implicitly load a chunk. Cycles terminate; destinations are deduplicated. A path through an unloaded region is absent for that update. A third controller is not a path bridge: its own later exchanges can transport energy, but it does not extend another pair's direct range.

Rebuild discovery each heat update rather than persist or cache adjacency between updates. GT vents are ordinary blocks with no addon callback, and fresh bounded scans avoid stale transfer after any placement/removal or chunk lifecycle event. This also avoids mixins and broad world block-change subscriptions. Check endpoint validity again immediately before each pair exchange. All discovery and mutation occurs synchronously on the server thread.

**Alternative rejected:** a geometric radius check permits wireless heat and long bent routes; a straight ray excludes requested branches. A long-lived topology cache requires more invalidation machinery than this first bounded network warrants.

### 4. One level-scoped coordinator and one package per controller pair

Propose `machines/shared/heat/HeatNetworkManager.java` with transient per-server-level loaded hatch positions, separately registered loaded heat-controller positions, and a Forge level-tick END handler. Register through one addon-owned Forge event subscriber rather than extending unrelated machine recipe ticks. Hatches and heat controllers register in `onLoad` and unregister in `onUnload`; clear level state on level unload and server shutdown. Store positions, not world-pinning long-lived controller references. Validate actual hatch/controller identity when resolving positions. The controller registry is independent of discovered connections so controllers without a usable hatch path still receive countdown processing. No persisted network graph or offline catch-up.

Use this one handler to enforce the confirmed ordering: first run the complete scheduled exchange stage when a transfer update is due, then finalize controller-owned melting countdowns once for that active game tick. Run countdown finalization on non-transfer ticks too. A separate earlier controller subscription must not destroy a machine before its final-tick exchange. The coordinator determines order only; energy, melting state, recovery, and remaining ticks remain authoritative controller state. Do not add an unscheduled exchange merely because a countdown is due, and do not delay expiry until the next scheduled transfer update.

Transfer cadence remains the existing proposed once-every-20-game-ticks schedule, aligned to the level clock. Coefficients are now tier requirements, not the old flat default:

| Hatch tier | Nominal coefficient, J/(K * update) |
| --- | ---: |
| HV | 2 |
| EV | 8 |
| IV | 32 |
| LuV | 128 |

The progression is fourfold per tier, ending at LuV. Every connection requests `kSender * temperatureDifference`, then applies the existing equilibrium and donor-energy caps. These are joules per kelvin per exchange update, not an absolute joule cap or an electrical rating. The confirmed mixed-tier policy uses only the currently sending hatch: LuV to HV uses 128 J/(K * update), while HV to LuV uses 2. Receiving tier does not impose an incoming limit, and becoming the donor selects that hatch's own coefficient rather than retaining the earlier sender's value.

Confirmed throughput rule: no multiplication from additional hatches or routes between the same controllers, but every distinct controller pair has an independent package allowance. More distinct peers can increase total transfer; do not add a shared per-hatch or per-controller throughput budget. No independent distance penalty within range is proposed.

Keep the cadence and explicit supported-tier coefficient mapping in the existing `common/Constants.java` rather than introducing a configuration subsystem. Reject unsupported tier construction instead of indexing an assumed contiguous full GT tier array or falling back to the old coefficient. The requested coefficients are requirements; do not silently retune them during verification.

On an update, discover eligible hatch connections with their endpoint tiers, group them by unordered controller pair, discard self-pairs, and sort pairs by stable controller block-position keys. Preserve endpoint-tier evidence when deduplicating; a set containing only controller positions would lose the information needed to select conductance. Process pairs sequentially using current heat values after each earlier pair and determine the current donor before selecting its sending coefficient. Retain the proposed non-stacking route reduction: select the highest sending-hatch coefficient among actual valid connections from that donor to this receiver, never sum coefficients or take the minimum of sender and receiver tiers. A higher-tier hatch without a valid path to this peer cannot supply the coefficient. Revalidate the selected connection before mutation and reevaluate sending-side selection whenever direction changes. This gives deterministic results independent of which hatch ticked first and prevents snapshot fan-out from overdrawing a donor. Do not claim instantaneous global equilibrium or mathematically order-independent redistribution: pairwise convergence is the intended model.

**Alternative rejected:** independent endpoint subscriptions can process each pair twice, and per-hatch throughput creates unintended parallel-hatch boosts. A global simultaneous optimizer adds complexity without a requested gameplay benefit.

### 5. Conservative transfer math without a safe-capacity admission cap

For the current hotter and cooler endpoints:

- `deltaT = Thot - Tcold`; if nonpositive or within a documented small numerical tolerance, do nothing.
- `sHot = (Mhot - 300) / Chot` and `sCold = (Mcold - 300) / Ccold`.
- `qEquilibrium = deltaT / (sHot + sCold)`.
- Resolve `kEffective` from the selected sending hatch's tier only; the receiving hatch contributes no conductance cap.
- `q = min(kEffective * deltaT, qEquilibrium, Qhot)`.

The equilibrium cap follows directly from setting `Thot - q*sHot = Tcold + q*sCold`. Capacity therefore affects energy needed for equilibration and the melting threshold, but never determines which side is hotter or rejects excess incoming heat. A receiver at its safe capacity still accepts a package from a hotter donor, stores all accepted energy, and enters melting if it crosses the threshold. Thermal equilibrium must not be overshot, but maximum safe temperature can be exceeded. No heat is discarded to protect the receiver.

Use the same positive, representable accepted amount for donor and receiver. Simulate mutations on both stores and recompute a common feasible amount based on donor energy and numerical representability, not safe receiver capacity. The base class mutation contract is final and runs on one server thread, with no external callbacks that can change acceptance between simulation and application. Validate actual accepted deltas; do not subtract a requested amount and then independently clamp the receiver. Reevaluate melting/recovery from the completed mutation without exploding midway through an energy exchange. Guard non-finite intermediate results, unrepresentably small changes, and floating-point endpoint rounding; do not introduce a minimum 1 J package. Specify a small K comparison tolerance for equilibrium and energy-relative conservation assertion tolerance in tests; these must not become a gameplay overcapacity allowance.

Arithmetic checked during planning with the clarified formula: `(C=1000 J, M=310 K, Q=500 J)` gives 305 K. Against `(C=2000 J, M=310 K, Q=0 J)`, the uncapped equilibrium exchange is approximately 333.333333 J and the common temperature approximately 301.666667 K. For same-tier hatch pairs the first packages are HV 10 J, EV 40 J, IV 160 J, and LuV approximately 333.333333 J, with LuV limited by equilibrium. These are arithmetic probes only, not tests of implementation that does not yet exist.

**Alternative rejected:** copying temperature percentages or averaging kelvin values ignores different energy-to-temperature slopes and does not conserve joules. Blindly transferring proportional heat without an equilibrium cap can oscillate. A receiver-capacity clamp would prevent the explicitly requested overheating and melting mechanic.

### 6. Controller-owned reversible melting state

Track and persist melting and the remaining 40-active-game-tick interval on the controller, not on hatches or transfer edges. The state machine is `safe -> melting -> exploded`, with a recovery transition `melting -> safe` when `Q <= C`. There are two explosion triggers while melting: expiry of the countdown and immediate destructive dismantling. The latter includes breaking any casing, the controller itself, or any attached heat hatch; casing-break references elsewhere in this design are examples, not exclusions of controller/hatch breaks. Crossing above capacity starts one countdown; further heat additions while still overheated do not restart it. Recovery clears the pending countdown entirely. A later crossing starts a fresh full countdown rather than resuming a remainder. A mere temperature decrease while `Q > C` does not recover the machine.

Evaluate thermal safety after real heat changes and after obtaining changed valid formed-structure characteristics. Melting does not prevent a valid formed machine from continuing heat exchange, including becoming a donor to a cooling machine. A simulated mutation changes neither state nor timer. Ordinary heat mutations start/update the countdown rather than exploding inline; destructive dismantling while melting is the explicit immediate exception and must not wait for the next periodic transfer update. Breaking an external connecting vent is not dismantling the multiblock: it invalidates the affected network paths, does not trigger immediate destruction, and does not pause/reset/cancel a still-ticking controller's melting countdown. Any remaining valid alternative paths continue to support exchange.

The countdown progresses once per active logical-server game tick independently of the proposed 20-tick transfer cadence and recipe activity. Invoke the controller's countdown finalization after that tick's complete scheduled exchange stage through the coordinator described above. Recheck current authoritative melting state before any expiry destruction: cooling to or below capacity on the final tick cancels the episode and fully resets the countdown. Insufficient cooling leaves the continuously overheated episode due for destruction that same tick, without an extra grace tick. Do not cache an expiring target before exchange and destroy it after recovery. If recovery is followed by renewed overheating, treat it as a fresh episode with its full interval, not the old expired deadline; preserve the full 40-active-tick interval for newly started episodes.

A controller without a currently connected vent path must still be able to reach its deadline; do not make countdown advancement depend on the manager discovering hatch pairs or on the entire structure being loaded. The controller owns the countdown: while it continues ticking, unloading another structure part or owned hatch does not pause the existing interval. Preserve upstream load/unload hooks and guard actual server execution. Pause while the controller itself is unloaded or the server is stopped, and restore the same remaining interval on resume. Use a persisted remaining-tick count, not a world-time deadline that includes offline time. Neither unload nor reload resets to a fresh interval, cancels melting, or triggers an offline explosion. Initial load/formation callbacks must not manufacture a recovery from temporarily unavailable characteristics. Immediate dismantling while still melting bypasses countdown finalization and does not wait for a hoped-for later exchange.

Distinguish actual casing/controller/attached-hatch destruction from ordinary chunk-unload, server-stop, and load/revalidation events before invoking destructive behavior. Do not place an unconditional explosion into a generic structure-invalidation hook: different causes can clear the same formation associations. Capture melting state, the controller's position, and its own hatch identities before controller removal or hatch detachment clears them. The formed-owner resolver used by exchange/UI cannot be the only source of destruction targets after invalidation. Include the original location of the controller or hatch being broken, so removing its block entity cannot bypass its explosion. The shared guarded explosion path must be able to finish even when the initial break removes the ticking controller. Dismantling after genuine cooling recovery does not invoke that rule. Missing/unloaded block data alone is not evidence of destructive dismantling.

When a deadline expires or destructive dismantling occurs during melting, destroy the affected controller and only its own heat hatches, with explosion sounds at the affected locations. Implement this as targeted block removal plus sound effects, not a damaging area explosion. Do not destroy additional casings, vents, unrelated blocks, or nearby machines; do not damage entities, apply knockback, or create fire. There is no blast radius or explosion-strength parameter to select. A casing already broken by the triggering action stays broken, but the failure effect does not destroy the remaining casings. Use the existing server-to-client sound mechanism rather than adding a custom packet or invoking an explosion solely to obtain its sound; no uninspected API signature is assumed here.

Do not walk the vent network to select other controllers' hatches. Capture and validate the intended own-hatch target set before destructive operations can clear multiblock associations, and prevent reentrant or repeated execution when targeted removal invokes further controller/hatch lifecycle callbacks. Guard against initiating another sequence without suppressing still-pending targets or duplicating sound effects in the original sequence. Breaking/replacing a controller or attached hatch while melting is not a safe detachment path; destruction uses the pre-break ownership. Handle temporarily unavailable targets and reload reconciliation as lifecycle bookkeeping under these requirements, without pausing a ticking controller, silently losing owned targets, or selecting unrelated replacement blocks. The exact bookkeeping mechanism must be grounded in the pinned loader's lifecycle and verified in the runtime fixture; no new gameplay choice is required.

**Alternatives rejected:** an irreversible fuse contradicts the confirmed cooling rescue; clamping away excess heat makes melting unreachable; restarting the fuse on every incoming package can postpone failure indefinitely. Allowing casing, controller, or attached heat hatch removal to pause/reset melting contradicts immediate destruction. Conversely, treating chunk unload or an external vent break as destructive dismantling would explode machines without the required trigger. A network-wide explosion contradicts the confirmed ownership scope, and a wall-clock/world-time deadline contradicts unload/restart pause.

### 7. Hatch-owned synchronized display snapshot

Display the hatch's immutable tier and nominal sending J/(K * update) coefficient separately from the synchronized controller snapshot. These remain meaningful when an owner is unavailable. Two differently tiered hatches on the same controller display the same owner thermal state but different tier/coefficient labels. Identify the coefficient as applying when sending heat, not as an incoming limit. Do not confuse nominal conductance with actual transferred energy or the controller's safe capacity.

Implement the normal GTCEu `createUIWidget()` entry point. Populate read-only display fields for owner status, stored J, structure-dependent safe capacity J, current K, maximum safe K, melting status, and remaining countdown ticks from the server owner resolver. Do not clamp overcapacity energy or above-limit temperatures in the UI. Use LDLib's existing managed-field synchronization on the hatch for this snapshot, with a non-persisted unavailable default and no mutation API. The snapshot is presentation only and is never used for heat calculations or explosion timing. Refresh on a short server-tick subscription registered in `onLoad`, guarded by an actual server level, and whenever owner association changes. `onUnload` cleanup must retain upstream behavior. Only changed values should be transmitted.

The client renders synchronized hatch values, not a lookup of the remote controller. This handles a long multiblock whose controller is outside client chunk tracking. Clear ordinary live-value snapshots on nondestructive loss of owner/formation and distinguish invalid thermal configuration from valid melting. Display a localized melting warning and ticking deadline while the owner is valid, clearing them on recovery. After reload, repopulate the snapshot from the preserved melting state and remaining interval rather than showing a fresh countdown. Casing-break destruction invalidates the affected hatch menu; it is not merely a harmless unavailable-owner transition. Add labels and statuses in `TrueSteamLang`, with units and display rounding; no chat-only replacement, input widgets, or heat controls. Reuse existing GTCEu/LDLib widgets; no standalone packet protocol is necessary.

**Alternative rejected:** client-side owner lookup relies on remote chunk availability; `@DescSynced` on the controller alone does not guarantee the hatch viewer receives it.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **Large dense vent volumes** → Search is depth-bounded and visited-set bounded, performed only on transfer updates. Include a dense/cyclic stress fixture and record scan cost. Optimize using per-update reuse only if measurement requires it; do not silently add a lower connectivity limit.
- **Floating-point drift and very large stores** → Preserve one shared transfer amount, validate finite slopes/intermediates, avoid integer rounding, and test conservation over repeated updates and near machine limits. Fail closed when a positive representable transfer cannot be made safely.
- **Sequential pair bias** → Stable order is reproducible and energy-safe; global fairness/instant equilibrium is intentionally not promised. Branch tests ensure all eligible pairs are considered.
- **Expiry races with last-chance cooling** → Use explicit exchange-then-countdown stages in the same level-tick coordinator, inspect current controller state after exchange, and test final-tick recovery/insufficient cooling regardless of controller or hatch registration order.
- **Stale owner references** → Central resolver verifies loaded world identity and formation; network scans never rely on UI snapshots or retained controllers.
- **No production heat users yet** → Test-only controllers and developer documentation establish usability without violating the instruction to leave current multiblocks unchanged.
- **Terminology confusion** → Label stored heat and maximum capacity separately in J, both temperatures in K, and document the ambient-relative formula; never present capacity as J/K.
- **Source guidance/build drift** → Use the checked-in ForgeGradle configuration as execution truth; no tooling migration is part of this feature.
- **Protective clamps hide required failure** → Audit mutation, persistence normalization, network admission, and UI independently so legitimate overcapacity energy survives each path and remains recoverable by heat exchange.
- **Unloading misclassified as damage** → Separate casing-break events from unavailable chunks and world shutdown; persist melting/countdown and test unload/restart without a spurious explosion or timer reset.
- **Reentrant destruction or unintended collateral damage** → Use one guarded targeted-removal-and-sound path for expiry and dismantling; capture own-hatch targets before destroying blocks, do not invoke a damaging area explosion, and verify remaining targets complete without duplicate removal/sound sequences.

## Migration Plan

1. On an explicit later apply request, implement additive heat classes, hatch registration/resources, tests, and integration documentation. Do not alter existing controller registrations or recipes.
2. Generate hatch resources from registration sources and verify with the pinned dependency set. New controllers default to zero stored heat; no existing save fields need migration.
3. Document how future controller subclasses derive capacity/max temperature from the structure, mutate joules, and allow the heat part ability in patterns. Explain front-face routing, range counting, per-pair throughput, update/conductance defaults, overcapacity melting, cooling rescue/reset, immediate casing-break destruction, unload/restart pause/resume, and that survival recipes are pack-provided.
4. Back up worlds before rolling back a release after placing new hatch/controller blocks; an older mod cannot preserve removed registry content. Remove or migrate those blocks before downgrade. Planning alone has no world or runtime migration effect.

## Remaining tuning and implementation considerations

Controller-owned timing, failure/ownership rules, and sender-only tier selection are settled; do not introduce whole-structure countdown gating or turn individual callback cases into new product requirements. The weaker-endpoint conductance recommendation was rejected: a lower-tier receiver does not throttle a higher-tier sender. The previously proposed strongest-valid-sending-connection reduction preserves the confirmed no-stacking rule without adding a shared throughput budget.

- **Transfer verification:** retain the existing proposed 20-tick cadence and the requested HV–LuV coefficients.
